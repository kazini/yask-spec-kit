# Document thinking

Use this when deciding how a document or instruction should read. `document-format.md` owns placement and short writing; `document-method.md` owns the change process.

## Format as meaning

Format is instruction, not decoration. A checklist signals verification, a decision tree signals branching, and sequential numbering implies order of operations. Changing format changes meaning; number only when order matters.

## Register and ambiguity

Match precision to the cognitive demand. The register of a passage is the level of precision its language signals, and so how much interpretation the reader should apply.

| Register | Use for | Pattern | If misused |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rigid | error prevention, boundaries, safety | MUST / NEVER / ALWAYS | brittle; pair with a constructive alternative |
| Conditional | procedure with flexibility | WHEN / IF / THEN | gaps where unlisted cases fall through |
| Invitational | judgment, exploration, adaptive work | CONSIDER / EXPLORE / ASSESS | drift: unfocused, chatty |
| Challenging | expose assumptions | QUESTION / CHALLENGE / RECONSIDER | friction: contrarian without cause |

Choose register per passage, not per document, and make transitions visible. Put safety and integrity first and rigid; put optimizations and enhancements after and invitational.

Ambiguity is a tool only when it is intentional: where judgment, context or exploration is wanted, open language invites engagement that rigid commands suppress. Test: can you say what the ambiguity is *for*? If not, it is a gap; close it. Its twin is purposeful vagueness — do not specify what need not be decided, record it as open, and add no detail ahead of need.

## Judgment in change

- Understand before restructuring: optimization starts from what the document enables.
- Preserve what enables, remove what constrains; tell helpful structure from bureaucratic overhead.
- Consolidate rather than eliminate: merge related ideas into one theme instead of deleting them.
- Prefer what works over what is elegant or comprehensive.

## Load and examples

Match structure to demand: clear commands for simple execution, structured frameworks for complex decisions, open guidance for creative work. Give an overview first, depth on demand, a consistent structure and one term per concept, defined once.

Give the reason with the rule in a short clause. A bare rule is followed to the letter and fails at its edges; a rule with its reason can be extended. Examples illustrate and do not replace principles: keep them short, inline and labelled, favour the case that shows reasoning, and let the rule win when they disagree.

## Self-monitoring in conversation

- Notice defaults to agreeableness, rigid directness or performative elaboration; ask what the situation actually requires.
- Calibrate before responding: **challenge** when the plan contradicts the goal or evidence, **accommodate** when the request is sound and cheap, **explore** when the goal is unclear, **direct** when the path is known.
- Pause before agreeing without analysis; at key moments check whether you are thinking about the problem or only its surface cues.
- Treat the user's style as context, not a template. Mirroring it to seem responsive is the style-mirroring trap; provide what the situation needs.

## Pitfalls

- Optimization trap: shortening without understanding purpose. Ask what the document enables before how to shorten it.
- Format rigidity trap: applying a format rule without asking whether it serves the purpose.
- Precision absolutism trap: treating maximum precision or maximum ambiguity as always better.
- Example proliferation trap: many examples where one distilled principle would carry across contexts.

## Worked cases

- **Safety-critical instructions.** Rigid boundaries with a constructive alternative: safety is non-negotiable, effectiveness needs an adaptive response.
- **Creative enhancement.** Invitational language and delegated judgment, with selective challenge so exploration stays focused.
- **Cross-document consistency.** Conditional pattern: when a fact changes, update its owner, then its pointers.
