# Document method

Use this when changing or reviewing a document. `document-thinking.md` explains the register choices, `document-format.md` owns placement and short writing.

## Two loops

- **Thinking loop.** Surface what is asked, the cognitive work needed, the deeper objective and how you are thinking; calibrate, act, then check whether the result served the deeper aim. Re-enter at the strategic question if it did not.
- **Document loop.** Understand purpose, readers and what would be lost; choose register and format per part; write with clean edit hygiene; verify links, coherence and a cold read; record the lesson. Exit when the change serves its purpose and verification passes.

## Change priorities

High-priority changes are things others depend on: decisions, interfaces, rules and broken links. Medium changes are refinements and internals; low changes are examples, formatting and cross-references after reorganizing. Timelines are project parameters, stated in `AGENTS.md` if wanted.

When a session loses time to stale, superseded or duplicated text, or a startup document is hard to read, run a cleanup pass before the new work: condense, de-duplicate, keep results and governing decisions.

## Proposing a change

A proposal states, in this order: existing coverage, cited as `file#Heading` after reading it in full (or what was searched); the delta — extends, replaces with reason, or new; the reason and evidence; impact on documents and components; and open questions. A proposal that corrects an earlier one says so. Mark unconfirmed proposals *(proposal)* and list them with pending decisions.

## Reading literally

- Read a document completely before you describe, extend, contradict or summarize it; headings, first lines and search hits are not reading. Say what you read in full and what you did not.
- Search before inventing. Use the documents' words, names and structures; do not propose as new what they already define.
- After context loss, re-read. Memory is not the source; verify claims with a search.
- Record the user's words first. A direction goes into the log in their words before it becomes a design, and their words win over paraphrase. Treat a correction as design input: when a choice is called limiting, widen it rather than defend it.
- Separate analysis from specification. Options and hypotheses live in an investigation; the specification states the chosen design. Claim only what you read or checked.
- Do not overreach. Leave undecided what need not be decided, record it as open, and add complexity only when a real problem requires it.
